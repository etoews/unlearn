Why you can't see it

  • Habit fires first
  • 3 prompts feel like proof
  • Losses loom larger

System 1 applies old patterns.

A 3-prompt vibe check passes a 1-in-4 failure 42% of the time.

Deleting risks a visible loss. Keeping hides a gain.

The rule you perfected is the hardest to drop.

  • "Habit fires first": "Thinking, Fast and Slow", Wikipedia, revision 8 Sep 2026 ("associating new information with existing patterns"). wikipedia.org

Judges have biases

  • Give it the answer
  • Long ≠ good
  • Pin it. Binary. Read why.

A reference answer cut false passes from 14/20 to 3/20.

Verbosity bias meets a verbose model.

Pin the judge. Pass/fail labels. Read the explanations.

Cites:

  • "Give it the answer": Zheng, L. et al., "Judging LLM-as-a-Judge with MT-Bench and Chatbot Arena", 2023 (wrong maths answers accepted: 14/20 default → 3/20 with reference). arXiv

  • "Binary": Husain & Shankar ("Binary evaluations force clearer thinking and more consistent labeling"). hamel.dev

Read results honestly

  • Run it again
  • Cost beside score
  • Evals expire too

Use repetitions. One run is noise.

Cheaper and buggier is a regression.

An eval lasts one to three generations.

Cites:

  • "Run it again": Anthropic, "Demystifying evals for AI agents" (variance across trials). anthropic.com · Arize Phoenix, repetitions. arize.com
